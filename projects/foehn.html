Foehn

Global probabilistic forecasting of air pollution, at kilometre scale.

arXiv preprint Interactive forecasts Code on GitHub

← All projects

Short-term exposure to fine particulate matter (PM2.5) accounts for about a million deaths a year. Operational forecasts run on grids tens of kilometres wide, which hide the pollution breathed by nearly two hundred million people a day. Foehn is a generative model that learns from global kilometre-scale estimates of PM2.5 and computes where the air comes from: it forecasts daily PM2.5 at 1 km over all inhabited land, up to a week ahead, as a calibrated ensemble.

Two ideas carry it. FLEXGREEN follows the air backwards through the three-dimensional weather forecast and turns the origins of the air into transport maps, with no learned parameter. A flow-matching network then forecasts continent-sized windows in one pass, with no tile seams, drawing possible futures instead of their blurred average.

Over a year of retrospective forecasts (2022), at thousands of stations never used to train or select the model, Foehn was more accurate than the operational forecast issued at the same time at three stations in four, and its ensemble mean detected local pollution extremes four days ahead as well as the operational forecast did one day ahead. I led the method, the experiments and the writing; the preprint is on arXiv (2610.25941), with Michael Boy and Zhi-Song Liu.

Key ideas

  1. Compute what physics knows. FLEXGREEN releases particles of air over every place on land and runs the weather forecast backwards, step by step, to find where that air was: a Green's function of the transport, computed once a day, with no learned parameter.
  2. Learn the rest. A generative network trained by flow matching turns noise into a kilometre-scale forecast, conditioned on the start day, the weather and the transport maps, over continent-sized windows in a single pass.
  3. Possible futures, not their average. Each forecast is an ensemble of four members drawn from different noise; fine-tuned on the CRPS, the ensemble is calibrated, so the peaks that matter for health are forecast instead of smoothed away.
  4. Validated where it was never trained. A year of retrospective forecasts (2022), scored at 6,324 stations of six official networks never used to train or select the model, against the operational forecast issued at the same time.
