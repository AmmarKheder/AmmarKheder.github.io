01 Latest preprint · October 2026 · with Michael Boy and Zhi-Song Liu

Foehn

Short-term exposure to fine particles accounts for about a million deaths a year, yet operational forecasts run on grids tens of kilometres wide, which hide the pollution breathed by nearly two hundred million people a day. Foehn, the model we present in this preprint, forecasts PM2.5 at 1 km over all inhabited land, up to a week ahead.

05 Profile

About

I am a doctoral researcher in computational engineering at LUT University in Lahti, with the Atmospheric Modelling Centre (AMC-Lahti) and INAR, University of Helsinki, supervised by Prof. Michael Boy and Assoc. Prof. Zhi-Song Liu. I try to combine what physics tells us about how the air travels with what generative networks can learn from data. Alongside the PhD, I work as a freelance AI research expert with Mercor, where I build scientific data-analysis workflows used to train AI agents that operate computers.

I am lucky to run these models on the LUMI supercomputer, on up to 1,024 AMD MI250X GPUs. Before the PhD: MSc in Engineering (Big Data & AI) at EiCnam Paris, with a one-year apprenticeship at INRIA Bordeaux in the Mnemosyne team (Frédéric Alexandre, Nicolas Rougier).

Topics air quality, atmospheric modelling, generative models (flow matching), Lagrangian transport, physics-informed neural networks, vision transformers, high-performance computing, large-scale distributed training.
