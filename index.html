About

I am a doctoral researcher in computational engineering at LUT University in Lahti, with the Atmospheric Modelling Centre (AMC-Lahti) and INAR, University of Helsinki, supervised by Prof. Michael Boy and Assoc. Prof. Zhi-Song Liu. My models compute what physics knows about how the air travels and learn the rest with generative networks.

I train them on the LUMI supercomputer, on up to 1,024 AMD MI250X GPUs. Before the PhD: MSc in Engineering (Big Data & AI) at EiCnam Paris, with a one-year apprenticeship at INRIA Bordeaux in the Mnemosyne team (Frédéric Alexandre, Nicolas Rougier).

Topics: air quality, atmospheric modelling, generative models (flow matching), Lagrangian transport, physics-informed neural networks, vision transformers, high-performance computing, large-scale distributed training.

Latest preprint · October 2026 · lead author

Foehn

Short-term exposure to fine particles accounts for about a million deaths a year, yet operational forecasts run on grids tens of kilometres wide, which hide the pollution breathed by nearly two hundred million people a day. Foehn, my latest model, forecasts PM2.5 at 1 km over all inhabited land, up to a week ahead.
